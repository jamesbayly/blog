Bukhara's old town is a hot brick warren - 2,500 years of settlement packed into an area you could walk across in twenty minutes, except that the streets were designed to stop you.

![The seat of the Khanate here](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-MPGcPmd/3/NfBLLTD36mTBsLTkVCTTN3H7sN9Zjpvrhz3FdZnjs/X3/DSC00713-X3.jpg)

They're built to confuse on purpose: dead ends, blind courtyards, walls with no windows facing the street at all. Privacy here has always been an architectural project in Islamic countries - family life happens behind a wall and inside a courtyard, never in view of a passerby from the wrong gender.

![A maze of streets](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-qNfd6ss/1/KZGzf6rfbxGVKhfVdsRnJtQmFJrgTJjqtMNdwfccR/X3/DSC00761-X3.jpg)

![Life hides in courtyards behind windows](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-HPd2F4z/1/NWTsMx63FbpDBbQ7Zd2NwQkwJzTQC45wFJhFXkKqj/X4/DSC00737-X4.jpg)

Even the doors keep the etiquette going: many still have two separate knockers, one for men and one for women, so whoever answers already knows who's outside before opening it. Hidden in the same tangle of lanes are abandoned madrasas most tour groups never reach - the maze hides both houses and classrooms.

![Kids playing football outside of the Modarykhan Madrasah](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-HDmSPTS/2/LkpsJDM9BtZcDWrRtMGzbcpSxZpd3JwPgxwgKSk27/X3/DSC00739-X3.jpg)

And it’s where we start to get hot after the thin mountain passes out east. Bukhara sits in the flattest, driest stretch of Uzbekistan, out on the true floor of the desert. Summers here regularly clear 40°C, and winters plunge below minus twenty. Almost no rain falls in either season.

![The walls of the Ark of Bukhara, a 5th centry fortress](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-8R2BjwQ/2/L8gkF5bt56tTpSpnQ584GkQdvgQqWhhgmNM3nSkzq/X3/DSC00708-X3.jpg)

The Zerafshan River is near - the same water that we followed from the Pamir mountains in Tajikistan. Bukhara runs on the last few drops of it, most siphoned off for cotton fields, the rest evaporated in a dry heat that makes the whole idea of privacy behind thick clay walls look like basic survival.

The city is built from rammed earth and mud - cheap, local, brutally practical in that seasonal swing from furnace to freezer. A wall like this needs rebuilding every few generations, so most of what looks eight hundred years old in these back lanes is really much younger, rebuilt on the same footprint over and over since some point in the Middle Ages.

![Decaying old rammed earth buildings](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-LvDCDpd/2/LbGQQK7Q5qQ5LrP2BcXV9xhPZDd23mG8d8fTMcKCK/X3/DSC00716-X3.jpg)

What is ancient is the timber - carved doors, ceiling beams, columns - passed down through however many walls have crumbled and gone up again around them. The skeleton is ancient.

![Old Timber is all that stands](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-D6nW7F5/2/MBKxL2wr6Nf67Q4Q7nrhTQktZ5GdWNLz4HXt4PRj6/X3/DSC00719-X3.jpg)

The Kalyan Minaret is the tallest thing around, built in 1127, and it did two jobs for most of its life: summon the faithful five times a day, and execute criminals by tossing them off the top. Locals still call it the Tower of Death.

![The tower of death](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-Kkp7sNV/2/NNgJLkwWTT79VjLZjTP3dMBg5JwXMgFLQNmPQ877D/X3/DSC00771-X3.jpg)

When Genghis Khan took the city in 1220 and flattened most of it, he looked up at the minaret, his hat fell off, he bent to pick it up, and decided anything that made him bow deserved to survive.

![The minaret that humbled Genghis Khan](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-9gfhGGk/2/KMCTbfdKH6xvcWnCqVhcFxhZfc5tFwWnQdd4xRL9W/X3/DSC00766-X3.jpg)

![](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-2q7dCfP/2/LLvmV3qp6ZmvkKNvsvdFhhLd9zKn9HbsDFdK3Fjkn/X3/DSC00768-X3.jpg)

The real business of the city happened in its caravanserais - walled courtyards with a single gated entrance, animals and goods stored below, merchants sleeping in cells around the upper level, built so a trader arriving after weeks in that same desert could feel safe behind a locked gate for one night before doing it all again. Within these embassic enclaves, communities of foreigners operated independently - one, home to generations of Hindu traders, kept their own customs, drank what the locals didn't, and took on the one job Islamic law wouldn't let Muslims do - moneylending. So did the city's Jews in another.

![Inside old Caravanserais](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-2bgFbmj/2/LnZfZFg52zRNv2BNvXrRMb7sdHLMsLkkBwPc6KLPC/X3/DSC00788-X3.jpg)

Today it's the same function, run at a smaller scale, for a different kind of traveler. The building that used to keep a silk merchant safe for the night now sells his modern equivalent a scarf to take home. The road just got shorter.

![The great Kalan Mosque, with 288 domes](https://photos.smugmug.com/Uzbekistan/i-ZTJnxfc/2/MdvWJmQ8c8NkqZX7JGqMZBLRTmhvQ4C2Z9LkJPvrk/X3/DSC00805-X3.jpg)
